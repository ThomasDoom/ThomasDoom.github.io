Power system for a Mars cave rover

NASA L’SPACE, Electrical Engineer, Sep - Dec 2025

What I did

I was the electrical engineer on a student team designing a rover to explore a cave on Mars, with no nuclear power allowed. I sized a 10,000 Wh lithium-sulfur battery and a sun-tracking solar array so the rover survives a 76-hour cave visit, 48 hours of it without sunlight. I ran the power trade studies, built the power budget across 13 operating modes, and wrote the power requirements. Through the challenges, I stepped up to leadership roles, kept the team on schedule and successfully presented our Preliminary Design Review to stakeholders.

How it connects to NASA

NASA’s Human Spaceflight Mission Directorate is building a Moon Base near the lunar South Pole and its Research and Technology Mission Directorate is developing fission surface power so crews have energy regardless of sunlight or temperature. My rover faced the same problem on a smaller scale: no sunlight underground and no nuclear option. NASA’s VIPER lunar rover was designed for similar ground, driving into permanently shadowed craters. Trade studies, power budgets and requirements like mine are core tasks for aerospace engineers.

YUBI, a local intelligence system

Personal project, Dec 2025 - present

What I did

YUBI is one central system I’m building to extend what I can do: it remembers, reasons and acts across my programs and devices. From the very first day, I’ve developed YUBI with the project lifecycle methods used by NASA. Before writing any code, I set 82 exit criteria across phased milestones and ran a failure modes and effects analysis. Each part has been developed through strict requirements and heavy testing, and today YUBI can hold conversations, keep a long-term memory, run a continuous thinking loop, and restart its own failed processes.

How it connects to NASA

Autonomous robots are one of the technologies NASA is advancing for the Moon Base. On the space station, Astrobee robots already work autonomously so astronauts spend less time on routine duties. YUBI is my testbed for that kind of crew support: a system built around one person’s work that can act on its own, the same goal behind the spacesuit displays college teams design for astronauts in NASA’s SUITS challenge. Building YUBI with NASA systems engineering habits makes requirements, testing, and failure analysis part of my daily practice.

Living and working off grid in Hawaii

Verto Education, Hawaii Field Semester, Fall 2021

What I did

In Fall 2021 I lived off grid in Kalapana, Hawaii, in screened cabins in the jungle an hour from the nearest city, learning from local Hawaiian families. As their guests I farmed in sacred valleys closed to outsiders, cleared the overgrown canal that waters one valley, and helped build an imu, an in-ground oven of lava rock and banana leaves, to cook a feast for a festival. I led hikes from the front or stayed back to carry anyone hurt or tired, organized and led a stargazing event for 100 students, and did community service clearing trails and restoring paths and gardens. I also looked after a young girl from one of the host families all semester and took thousands of photos for the group, which connected me to everyone.

How it connects to NASA

NASA lists teamwork and inclusion among its core values, and NCAS staff said teamwork and communication can come from any experience, not only a job. HI-SEAS, a Mars-simulation habitat on Mauna Loa on the island where I lived, has new NASA funding to resume its missions, and NASA’s own CHAPEA crews spend 378 days in a 3D-printed Mars habitat at Johnson. Hawaii gave me that at a small scale: a group living off grid, far from help, relying on each other and growing its own food, which NASA is researching for the Moon and Mars, where crops would supply food and oxygen. Learning from the Hawaiian families, on their land and by their rules, taught me to listen first and work as part of someone else’s team.

Degree

A bachelor’s in engineering from a school with at least one ABET-accredited engineering program, or in physical science, math or computer science. Aerospace engineering and engineering physics qualify directly for AST titles; electrical engineering qualifies with nine semester hours in subjects such as mechanics, dynamics or thermodynamics. NASA’s spring 2027 Pathways openings took aerospace, aeronautical and astronautical majors for aerospace roles and electrical or electronics majors for electrical roles; engineering physics was listed only at the doctorate level. Most aerospace engineering jobs need a four-year degree.

Finish my A.S. in Electrical Engineering at Columbia State in August 2027, then transfer in Fall 2027 to an ABET-accredited B.S. in aerospace engineering or engineering physics. If I stay in electrical engineering, Statics and Dynamics count toward those mechanics hours.

Entry level (GS-7)

GS-7 is the lowest grade on the federal pay scale in NASA’s engineering postings. Most applicants qualify on GPA: 2.9 or better, or the upper third of the class. The other route is a year of engineering support work, such as collecting data, keeping records and preparing technical documentation.

4.00 GPA now; keep it above 2.9 through the B.S. On L’SPACE I collected the power data, kept the budgets and wrote the requirements and review documents. Hands-on testing is what I still need, and NASA internships give it.

Teamwork and reporting

Directing engineering staff and writing technical reports are core aerospace engineer tasks. Senior AST engineers provide technical mentorship and discipline leadership.

On L’SPACE I kept the team on schedule after 70% of it left and presented our design review. I’m taking Fundamentals of Communication (COMM 2025) this semester.

Applying

A federal resume of at most two pages, with the month and year of every job, and a transcript that shows GPA. Experience must be described in your own words.

A way in

OSTEM internships are paid and need U.S. citizenship and a 3.0 GPA; summer 2027 applications close March 1, 2027. Community college students are eligible, and NCAS participants qualify for MUREP-funded internships. Pathways leads directly to full-time jobs. NASA Force, a NASA hiring program, brings in early-career engineers on 1 to 2 year terms for work like VIPER rover operations. Outside NASA, the Air Force’s Palace Acquire program hires new aerospace and electrical engineers into a 3-year paid training plan that ends in a permanent job.

Apply to every summer 2027 OSTEM project I qualify for on NASA STEM Gateway, NASA’s internship application site, before the March 1 deadline, and name NCAS in each one. Apply to Pathways on USAJOBS early in the B.S., and NASA Force after it.

Astronaut path, long term

A master’s in engineering, science, computer science or math, at least three years of related professional experience after it, vision correctable to 20/20, and medical, fitness and swim tests. NASA’s open astronaut candidate posting closes December 3, 2026, and the next class is expected in fall 2027.

After the B.S., work as an engineer, earn a master’s, then apply.
